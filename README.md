
# Customer Segmentation Analysis using K-Means Clustering

## Project Overview

Developed a Customer Segmentation Dashboard to identify distinct customer groups based on annual income and spending behavior. Using K-Means Clustering, customers were segmented into meaningful groups to help businesses understand purchasing patterns, improve customer engagement, and design targeted marketing strategies.

## Key Insights

### Customer Segmentation
- Successfully segmented customers into **5 distinct clusters** based on annual income and spending behavior.
- Customer groups exhibit significantly different purchasing patterns and engagement levels.

### High-Value Customers
- High-income customers demonstrate varied spending behavior, indicating multiple opportunities for personalized marketing.
- Cluster 0 contains the highest-spending customers and represents a valuable target segment for premium products and loyalty programs.

### Low-Engagement Customers
- Cluster 1 consists of customers with relatively lower engagement and spending activity.
- This segment may benefit from targeted promotions and retention campaigns.

### Customer Distribution
- Cluster 0 is the largest customer segment, accounting for approximately **40.5%** of the customer base.
- Most customers belong to mid-spending segments, indicating balanced purchasing behavior across the population.

### Income vs Spending Analysis
- Customer spending is not directly proportional to income.
- Several high-income customers display low spending behavior, highlighting opportunities for targeted engagement and personalized offers.

---

## Business Recommendations

- Focus marketing efforts on high-value customer segments to maximize revenue.
- Design loyalty and premium membership programs for high-spending customers.
- Develop personalized campaigns to improve engagement among low-spending segments.
- Use customer segmentation insights to optimize product recommendations and promotional strategies.
- Continuously monitor customer behavior to identify changes in purchasing patterns.
